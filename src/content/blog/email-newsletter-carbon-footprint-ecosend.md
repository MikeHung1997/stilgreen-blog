# Does Your Email Newsletter Have a Carbon Footprint? (It Does — Here's What to Do About It)

You optimized your subject lines. You A/B tested your CTAs. You cleaned your list and got your open rates up.

But here's the question almost no marketer asks: how much CO₂ did that campaign emit?

It sounds like a strange question. Email feels weightless — no paper, no printing, no trucks. But every email you send travels through data centers that consume enormous amounts of electricity. The servers, the cooling systems, the network infrastructure — all of it runs on power, and most of that power still comes from fossil fuels.

For a small newsletter, the impact is minor. But if you're sending to tens of thousands of subscribers on a weekly basis, your email marketing has a measurable carbon footprint — and it adds up faster than most people realize.

---

## Why This Is Starting to Matter More

The global energy picture is shifting. As geopolitical pressures continue to disrupt oil supply chains — a pattern we've seen play out repeatedly since the oil crises of the 1970s — the cost and reliability of fossil-fuel-dependent infrastructure becomes more volatile. Businesses that rely on energy-intensive digital tools are quietly exposed to that volatility.

At the same time, audiences are paying closer attention to the environmental values of the brands they support. A growing segment of consumers and B2B buyers actively prefer working with companies that take sustainability seriously — and that includes how those companies operate digitally.

Green email marketing isn't a niche anymore. It's becoming a differentiator.

---

## The Problem With Most Email Marketing Platforms

The major email platforms — Mailchimp, Klaviyo, ConvertKit, ActiveCampaign — are powerful tools. But none of them were built with carbon footprint in mind. They run on standard cloud infrastructure, they don't track your campaign emissions, and they certainly don't offset them.

This doesn't make them bad products. But it does mean that if sustainability is part of your brand, your email tool might be quietly working against your message.
