If you've been looking at portable power stations, you've probably landed on EcoFlow and Jackery. They're the two dominant brands, they're both well-reviewed, and they both make solid products.

So which one should you actually buy?

The honest answer: it depends on what you're using it for. Here's how they compare across the things that actually matter.

## Capacity and output

Both brands offer a wide range of sizes, from small units that can charge your phone and laptop to large home backup systems. EcoFlow's lineup tends to offer higher wattage output at similar price points, which matters if you're running power-hungry appliances.

Jackery focuses more on the camping and outdoor market, with units optimized for portability and ease of use rather than maximum output.

## Charging speed

This is where EcoFlow has a clear advantage. Their X-Stream charging technology can charge compatible units from 0 to 80% in under an hour. Jackery's charging speeds are more conventional — adequate, but not a selling point.

If you're using a power station for emergencies or want to top it up quickly between uses, EcoFlow wins this category.

## Solar compatibility

Both brands have their own solar panel ecosystems, and both work with third-party panels. Jackery's SolarSaga panels are popular for camping because they're lightweight and fold compactly. EcoFlow's panels are generally more efficient but heavier.

## Price

At comparable capacity, EcoFlow and Jackery are usually within 10-15% of each other in price. EcoFlow tends to run sales more aggressively, so checking current pricing before buying is worth it.

## Who should buy which

**Choose EcoFlow if:**
- You want the fastest charging speeds
- You're using it for home backup or higher-wattage appliances
- Fast recharging between uses matters to you

**Choose Jackery if:**
- You're primarily using it for camping and outdoor activities
- Portability and weight are priorities
- You prefer a simpler, more established product lineup

Both brands offer solid warranties and good customer support. You won't make a wrong choice with either — but knowing which fits your use case will save you money and frustration.
