The short answer is yes. The longer answer is: it depends on where you live, what you're buying, and whether you're accounting for the full cost correctly.

Here's what the data actually shows.

## Solar panels: genuinely cheaper

The cost of solar panels has dropped dramatically over the past decade. What used to cost tens of thousands of dollars for a home system now starts at a fraction of that — and portable solar solutions for apartments and renters have made the technology accessible to people who don't own their homes.

The catch: installation costs haven't dropped at the same rate as panel costs. If you're doing a full rooftop system, labor and permits still add significant expense. Portable and plug-in solar setups sidestep this entirely, which is why they've grown so fast.

## Home batteries: getting there, but not cheap yet

Whole-home battery systems like the popular options from major manufacturers remain expensive. The math works if you're in an area with high electricity rates or frequent outages — but for most households in stable grids, the payback period is still long.

Smaller portable power stations are a different story. For camping, emergencies, or powering specific devices, the cost-per-use math is much more favorable.

## The real question: what are you actually trying to solve?

Green energy isn't one decision — it's a series of smaller ones. A portable solar panel for your balcony. A power station for outages. An EV charger for your garage. Each of these has a different cost curve and a different payback timeline.
