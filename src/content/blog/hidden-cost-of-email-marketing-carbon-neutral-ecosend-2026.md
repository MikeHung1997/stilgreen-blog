You've optimized your subject lines. You've A/B tested your CTAs. You've segmented your list down to the last decimal point.

But there's one thing most email marketers have never thought to optimize: the carbon cost of sending.

It sounds abstract. But the internet's infrastructure — the servers, the data centers, the network equipment that routes your newsletter to 10,000 inboxes — runs on electricity. A lot of it. And in most cases, that electricity still comes from fossil fuels.

The emissions from a single email are tiny. Multiply that by the volume of a typical marketing operation, and you're looking at a footprint that's harder to ignore — especially in 2026, when ESG reporting is becoming a baseline expectation for businesses of almost any size.

## What's Actually Happening Behind Every Send

Email marketing feels lightweight. No packaging. No shipping. No physical waste.

But every campaign you send triggers a chain of energy consumption: your email platform's servers process the send, data centers store and route each message, recipient devices download and render your content, and tracking pixels fire every time someone opens.

Estimates vary, but the data center industry as a whole accounts for roughly 1–2% of global electricity use — and that figure is rising as digital marketing volume grows. For high-frequency senders or large-list operators, the cumulative emissions are meaningful.

More importantly, they're now measurable. And if they're measurable, they're reportable. And if they're reportable, they're a liability — or an opportunity, depending on how you act.
