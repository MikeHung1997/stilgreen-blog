## What Actually Matters During a Power Outage

Not all outages are created equal. A two-hour flicker after a thunderstorm is annoying. A 36-hour outage in winter, or during a heat wave, is a genuine health risk.

What you need to keep running in an emergency usually falls into three categories:

**Medical and safety**: CPAP machines, medical refrigerators, lights, phone charging for emergency communication.

**Food preservation**: A standard refrigerator runs between 100–400W. The longer the outage, the more this matters.

**Basic comfort**: Fans, small heaters, laptop or tablet for remote work or keeping kids occupied.

Once you know what you're trying to power, capacity and output wattage become the two numbers that matter most.

## Why Capacity and Charging Speed Are Both Critical

Capacity tells you how long you can run things. Output tells you what you can run at once. But there's a third factor that most buyers overlook: **how fast can you recharge it?**

If your outage lasts multiple days, or if you're using the unit for camping trips and want it ready to go again by morning, recharge time becomes just as important as the battery size.
