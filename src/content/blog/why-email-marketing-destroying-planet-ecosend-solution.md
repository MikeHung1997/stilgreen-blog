# Why Your Email Marketing Is Secretly Destroying the Planet (And How to Fix It)

You probably didn't wake up this morning thinking your email newsletter was contributing to climate change. But here's the uncomfortable truth: every email you send has a carbon footprint.

Data centers powering email platforms consume massive amounts of electricity. According to recent studies, the ICT sector accounts for approximately 3-4% of global carbon emissions—more than the aviation industry. Your weekly newsletter? It's part of that equation.

The average marketing email generates about 4 grams of CO2. Multiply that by thousands of subscribers and monthly campaigns, and suddenly you're looking at tons of carbon emissions annually. For businesses sending millions of emails, the environmental impact becomes staggering.

## The Hidden Environmental Cost of Digital Marketing

Most marketers don't realize that their email service provider's infrastructure runs on fossil fuels. Traditional platforms like Mailchimp, Constant Contact, and SendGrid operate massive server farms that require constant cooling and power—most of it from non-renewable sources.

Every time you:
- Send a campaign to 10,000 subscribers
- Store customer data on cloud servers
- Run A/B tests across segments
- Archive old campaigns

You're contributing to energy consumption that likely comes from coal or natural gas power plants.

This isn't about guilt. It's about awareness and better choices.
